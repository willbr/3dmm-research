# Movie-making manifesto

Rules learned from recreating reference footage with props and MCP, with filmmaking guidance from the [archived 3dmm Manifesto](3dmm%20Manifesto.html).

Reference exercises prioritise fidelity; original films should develop their own dramatic and visual ideas.

1. **Measure proportions first.** Compare full-body, profile and frontal views. Fix anatomy before fitting cameras around it.
2. **Build a coherent set.** Place doors and windows on the correct walls. Leave room for entrances, exits and furniture.
3. **Make camera choices deliberate.** Match reference position, perspective, crop, movement and cut timing from start to finish. For original work, motivate camera changes; static shots are valid.
4. **Block for readability and depth.** Match facing directions and eyelines; keep important actors and gestures visible. Use depth and background reactions to guide attention.
5. **Animate the whole action.** Move shoulders, elbows, wrists and fingers together. Include torso movement, weight shifts, head turns and reactions.
6. **Keep contact believable.** Plant feet, attach ears, grip objects and check clearance around walls, furniture and foliage.
7. **Follow the actual design.** Check clothing from every visible angle. Build facial expression from the reference's shapes; avoid invented features.
8. **Separate hands from props.** Use shape, colour and staging so a parcel, garment and fingers remain distinguishable.
9. **Preserve continuity.** Keep scale, handedness, carried objects and screen direction consistent. A turn must not accidentally reposition the actor.
10. **Bake efficiently and compatibly.** Use script-side groups and joints, export ordinary retail-readable events, and avoid redundant keys for newly created actors. When replacing existing animation, overwrite every affected frame so old keys cannot restore previous poses or scenery.
11. **Review the saved movie.** Compare frames and watch complete shots in motion. Inspect intermediate poses and transitions. Passing runtime tests does not prove a visual match.
12. **Protect the originals.** Keep references read-only, preserve earlier versions, and put experiments under ignored `tmp/`. Keep copyrighted test media out of Git.
13. **Give the film a purpose.** Decide what the viewer should understand or feel. Give characters motives and make their changes believable.
14. **Plan before building.** Study effective film scenes, write the scene beats, and sketch key shots before constructing or animating them.
15. **Tell the story visually.** Show emotion and intent through composition, performance and colour. Let dialogue support what the audience can see.
16. **Direct sound and performances.** Use music and effects that fit the scene, keep dialogue intelligible, and direct or retake voice performances.
17. **Edit for purpose and pace.** Keep scenes manageable to revise. Cut, shorten or rebuild material that does not serve the story or mood.
